# Dynamic Pricing Sentence Templates

Sentence patterns for dynamic pricing and revenue management papers.

---

## [Data-Rich Environment] We analyze [algorithm] in a "data-rich environment" where [additional information] is available.

**Source**: Qiang & Bayati - "Dynamic Pricing with Demand Covariates" (arXiv 2016)
**Context**: Motivating problem with rich data structure

> We analyze a greedy iterative least squares (GILS) approach in a "data-rich environment" where historical demand information and covariates are available.

**Tags**: #motivation #data #pricing #or

---

## [Surprising Finding] We demonstrate a counterintuitive finding: even [seemingly useless modification] would make [algorithm] [desirable property].

**Source**: Qiang & Bayati - arXiv 2016
**Context**: Highlighting unexpected theoretical result

> We demonstrate a counterintuitive finding: even adding uninformative covariates to the original dynamic pricing problem would make GILS asymptotically optimal.

**Tags**: #contribution #surprising #theory #pricing

---

## [Online Marketplace Motivation] The paper addresses [setting] with [challenge]. The core challenge involves [learning problem] while dynamically [optimization goal].

**Source**: Javanmard & Nazerzadeh - "Dynamic Pricing in High-dimensions" (arXiv 2016)
**Context**: Framing problem for online platforms

> The paper addresses a firm selling numerous products with high-dimensional feature representations to arriving customers. The core challenge involves learning unknown choice model parameters from binary sales data while dynamically setting prices to minimize regret.

**Tags**: #motivation #online #marketplace #pricing

---

## [Choice Model Setup] We assume customer purchasing follows a general choice model incorporating [factors], all encoded as [representation].

**Source**: Javanmard & Nazerzadeh - arXiv 2016
**Context**: Setting up demand model

> We assume customer purchasing follows a general choice model incorporating product features, customer characteristics, and offered prices—all encoded as numerical vectors.

**Tags**: #model #setup #choice #pricing

---

## [Algorithm + Bounds] We present [algorithm name], achieving [upper bound] regret. We simultaneously establish a matching lower bound of [lower bound], demonstrating near-optimal performance.

**Source**: Javanmard & Nazerzadeh - arXiv 2016
**Context**: Stating algorithmic contribution with optimality

> We present the Regularized Maximum Likelihood Pricing (RMLP) algorithm, achieving O(s₀ log d · log T) regret. We simultaneously establish a matching lower bound of O(s₀ (log d + log T)), demonstrating near-optimal performance.

**Tags**: #contribution #algorithm #bounds #pricing

---

## [Learning vs Earning] The firm must simultaneously set prices to maximize revenue while learning the underlying [structure]. This is the fundamental [exploration/learning]-[exploitation/earning] tradeoff.

**Source**: Multiple dynamic pricing papers
**Context**: Describing core tradeoff

> The firm must simultaneously set prices to maximize revenue while learning the underlying demand structure. This is the fundamental learning-earning tradeoff.

**Tags**: #tradeoff #learning #earning #pricing

## [Myopic Policy Failure] Using myopic policy will lead to incomplete learning and, thus, poor profit performance.

**Source**: Keskin & Zeevi (2014) - cited in multiple works
**Context**: Motivating need for exploration

> As pointed out by Keskin and Zeevi (2014), using myopic policy will lead to incomplete learning and, thus, poor profit performance.

**Tags**: #motivation #myopic #exploration #pricing

**Context**: Defining regret benchmark

> The seller aims to minimize its pseudo-regret, i.e., the expected revenue loss relative to a clairvoyant who knows the underlying demand model.

## [Sufficient Condition] [Paper] provided a generally sufficient condition for [method] which ensures that regret does not exceed the order of [rate].

**Context**: Stating theoretical guarantee

> Keskin and Zeevi (2014) provided a generally sufficient condition for iterated least squares methods which ensures that regret does not exceed the order of √T.
