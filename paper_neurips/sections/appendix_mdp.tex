\section{General MDP Formulation of Noise Trajectory Search}
\label{sec:mdp}

This appendix has two purposes. First, it presents a general Markov decision process (MDP) for inference-time search over diffusion-noise trajectories, extending the simpler formulation used in the main text. Second, it uses this MDP as a unified language for classifying ten prior inference-time-search methods (\cref{tab:mdp_policies}) and locating where \textsc{GAINS} sits among them.

The main-text framework (\cref{sec:framework}) uses a restricted policy: the sampler advances through timesteps in a single forward pass, and each timestep runs the same local search operator (e.g., random search) over its noise draw. The general MDP introduced here lifts both restrictions, allowing (i) multiple verifier types of different cost, (ii) backtracking to revisit earlier timesteps, and (iii) different search operators at different timesteps. \citet{ramesh2025nts} first cast diffusion sampling as an MDP with state $(c, x_t, t)$ and terminal reward $r(x_0, c)$, then relaxed it to independent contextual bandits for tractability. We extend this formulation in four respects: the state includes the search history $\mathcal{H}_t$ and the remaining compute budget $R$; the action set is partitioned into Search, Verify, and Move categories (defined below); the reward admits weighted combinations of multiple verifier scores; and we use the resulting MDP to classify ten existing methods as special-case policies.

\paragraph{State space.}
At each decision step, the agent observes $s = (c, t, x_t, \mathcal{H}_t, R)$. Here $c$ is the prompt or other conditioning signal, $t\in\{1,\dots,T\}$ indexes the current diffusion timestep (counting down from $T$ to $0$), $x_t$ is the current latent at that timestep, $\mathcal{H}_t$ records the search history at $t$ (past candidate noises, verifier scores, and actions taken), and $R\in\{0,\dots,B\}$ is the remaining compute budget measured in number of function evaluations (NFE) of the diffusion network. The initial state is $s_0=(c, T, x_T, \emptyset, B)$ with $x_T\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, and the episode terminates when either $t=0$ (a clean sample is reached) or $R=0$ (the budget is exhausted).

\paragraph{Action space.}
The action set partitions into three categories, $\mathcal{A}(s) = \mathcal{A}_{\text{search}}\cup\mathcal{A}_{\text{verify}}\cup\mathcal{A}_{\text{move}}$, distinguished by whether they propose a noise candidate, score it, or commit to a transition:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item \emph{Search} ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{search}}$): one iteration of a local search operator over the noise variable $\varepsilon_t$ at the current timestep $t$, without changing $t$ or $x_t$. Variants include \textsc{RandomSearch} (draw $\varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, keep if it scores higher than the incumbent), \textsc{EpsGreedy} (with probability $\epsilon$ explore via a fresh Gaussian draw, otherwise exploit by perturbing the incumbent), \textsc{ZeroOrder} (a zero-order finite-difference gradient step on the noise variable), and \textsc{Langevin} (one Langevin MCMC step over the noise variable). Cost: 1 NFE per iteration.
  \item \emph{Verify} ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{verify}}$): evaluate the current $x_t$ or its one-step prediction of the clean sample $\hat x_0$, without modifying the latent. \textsc{VerifyLight} reuses the already-computed $\hat x_0$ and applies a cheap verifier (e.g., JPEG compressibility, pixel-level statistics, or the LAION aesthetic predictor) at 0 additional NFE. \textsc{VerifyHeavy} runs additional passes of the diffusion denoising network to refine the $\hat x_0$ estimate before applying the verifier, at $\ge 1$ NFE.
  \item \emph{Move} ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{move}}$): commit to a timestep transition. \textsc{Forward} sets $x_{t-1}=F_\theta(x_t,t,c,\varepsilon_t^*)$ using the best noise candidate $\varepsilon_t^*$ found so far at $t$ and advances the index from $t$ to $t-1$. We charge \textsc{Forward} at $0$ NFE: the forward pass through the denoising network has already been performed when $\varepsilon_t^*$ was scored as a \textsc{Search} candidate, and \textsc{Forward} merely commits the cached latent. \textsc{Backtrack} returns from $t$ to $t+1$, restores the previously saved latent, retains $\mathcal{H}_t$, and also costs 0 NFE. With this convention the per-step NFE accounting matches the main text: a step at which $K_t$ \textsc{Search} actions are taken (followed by one \textsc{Forward}) consumes exactly $K_t$ NFE.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Transition dynamics and reward.}
The transitions follow directly from the action descriptions: after a \textsc{Search} action, $t$ and $x_t$ are unchanged, $\mathcal{H}_t$ is augmented with the new candidate, and $R$ decreases by 1; after \textsc{Forward}, $t$ decreases by 1, $x_{t-1}$ is set to $F_\theta(x_t,t,c,\varepsilon_t^*)$, and $R$ is unchanged; after \textsc{Backtrack}, $t$ increases by 1, $x_{t+1}$ is restored from saved history, and $\mathcal{H}$ is retained. The reward is non-zero only at termination: $r(s_{\text{terminal}})=v(x_0,c)$, where $v$ is a verifier score on the final clean sample (weighted combinations $v=\alpha v_1 + \beta v_2$ of multiple verifiers are also allowed). The agent maximizes expected terminal score, $\max_\pi \mathbb{E}[v(x_0,c)]$, subject to the budget constraint $R\ge 0$ throughout.

\paragraph{Existing methods as MDP policies.}
\cref{tab:mdp_policies} classifies prior inference-time-search methods as policies in the MDP defined above. Three structural patterns emerge. (i)~\emph{No method mixes verifier granularity:} all methods commit a priori to either a cheap or an expensive verifier; adaptively switching between \textsc{VerifyLight} and \textsc{VerifyHeavy} based on state remains unexplored. (ii)~\emph{Backtracking is rare:} only ABCD and tree-search methods ever return to an earlier timestep $t+1$ from $t$. (iii)~\emph{Operator selection is static:} each listed method fixes one search operator (random, $\epsilon$-greedy, zero-order, or Langevin) for the entire trajectory rather than choosing per timestep.

\paragraph{Main-text formulation as a special case.}
The two-level framework of \cref{sec:framework} and the \textsc{GAINS} algorithm (\cref{alg:gains}) correspond to a restricted policy class within this MDP. The restrictions are: no backtracking, a single search operator throughout the trajectory, a single verifier, and a factored budget rule in which the total budget $B$ is pre-partitioned across timesteps by offline profiling and then adjusted online within a small slack $\delta$. In MDP terms, the policy's dependence on the history $\mathcal{H}_t$ collapses to two running summaries at the current timestep: the running mean gain $\overline{g}^{\,\mathrm{run}}$ (improvement of the verifier score so far) and the running mean of within-step empirical variance $\overline{\sigma}^{\,\mathrm{run}}$ (dispersion of candidate scores so far; distinct from the timestep-average $\bar\sigma$ of theoretical sensitivities in \cref{thm:offline}). Relaxing these restrictions yields richer policy classes inside the same MDP that may further improve the quality-compute frontier, but learning such policies likely requires reinforcement learning rather than the closed-form scheduling rules of the main text.
