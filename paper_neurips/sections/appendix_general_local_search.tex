\section{Extension to General Local Search Operators}
\label{sec:appendix-general}
%
This appendix extends the scheduling theory of \cref{sec:theory} from random search to a broader family of local search operators. The main-text analysis instantiates the local operator as pure random search (i.i.d.\ Gaussian noise draws, keep-best). The scheduling results, however, only use three properties of the resulting gain sequence: it is increasing in the per-step budget $K$, its marginal increments do not accelerate, and its functional form does not depend on the diffusion timestep $t$. Any local search operator that produces a gain sequence with these three properties inherits the same offline water-filling allocation and the same Jensen-gap argument for online adaptation. Below we make this observation precise. We first state four regularity conditions on a generic local operator (\cref{asm:local-search}), prove a general gain factorization (\cref{thm:general-gain}), transfer the offline and online scheduling results (\cref{cor:offline-general,cor:online-general}), and then verify the conditions for $\epsilon$-greedy exploration and local perturbation search alongside random search.
%
\subsection{Abstract Local Search Framework}
\label{sec:abstract-ls}
%
This subsection sets up a generic local search operator and the gain it produces, then states the four regularity conditions (A1)--(A4) that the rest of the appendix needs.

A local search operator $\mathcal{L}$ runs at a fixed diffusion timestep $t$ and produces $K$ candidate noise iterates $\xi^{(1)}, \ldots, \xi^{(K)}$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$, each evaluated through the diffusion transition. Random search draws each $\xi^{(j)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ independently; iterative operators ($\epsilon$-greedy, local perturbation) may make $\xi^{(j+1)}$ depend on $\xi^{(1)},\ldots,\xi^{(j)}$. The \emph{gain} from $K$ iterations measures how much the best-of-$K$ verifier score exceeds the no-search baseline $\mu_t$:
\begin{equation}
  G_t^{\mathcal{L}}(K) := \mathbb{E}\Bigl[\max_{1 \le j \le K} \Psi_t\bigl(\bar{X}_{t-h} + g_t\sqrt{h}\,\xi^{(j)};\,c\bigr)\,\Big|\,X_t,c\Bigr] - \mu_t,
  \label{eq:gain-general-app}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_t = \Psi_t(\bar{X}_{t-h}; c)$ is the score with no search (as in the main text), and we adopt the convention $G_t^{\mathcal{L}}(0) := 0$. This convention aligns with the main-text accounting: a step that consumes $K_t$ NFE evaluates exactly $K_t$ noise candidates. The next assumption collects four properties that suffice for every result in this appendix.
%
\begin{assumption}[Local search regularity]
\label{asm:local-search}
Define the projected gain sequence
\begin{equation}
  \phi_K^{\mathcal{L}} := \mathbb{E}\bigl[\max\nolimits_{1 \le j \le K} \langle u, \xi^{(j)}\rangle\bigr]
  \label{eq:phi-def}
\end{equation}
for any unit direction $u$ (the value is shown to be $u$-independent below); set $\phi_0^{\mathcal L} := 0$. A local search operator $\mathcal{L}$ satisfies:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textup{(A1)}] \emph{Strict monotone improvement.} $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$ is strictly increasing in $K$ for $K \ge 1$.
\item[\textup{(A2)}] \emph{Diminishing returns.} The marginal gains $\Delta\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}} := \phi_{K+1}^{\mathcal{L}} - \phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$ are non-increasing for $K \ge 1$ (equivalently, $\phi_K^{\mathcal L}$ is discretely concave on $K \ge 1$). (We exclude $K=0$ because $\phi_1^{\mathcal L}=\phi_0^{\mathcal L}=0$ for operators initialized from a zero-mean isotropic distribution $\xi^{(1)}\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$, so $\Delta\phi_0=0$ while $\Delta\phi_1>0$; the downstream budget constraint $K_t \ge 1$ keeps $K=0$ outside the feasible region.)
\item[\textup{(A3)}] \emph{Direction independence.} The expected projected gain
\[
  \mathbb{E}\Bigl[\max_{1 \le j \le K}\langle u, \xi^{(j)}\rangle\Bigr]
\]
does not depend on the unit direction $u \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ (this follows from (A4) for the operators we consider; we list it separately because it is the property used in proofs).
\item[\textup{(A4)}] \emph{Rotational invariance of the joint law.} For any orthogonal matrix $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$, the joint distribution of $\{\xi^{(j)}\}_{1 \le j \le K}$ under $\mathcal{L}$ is invariant under the simultaneous rotation $\xi^{(j)} \mapsto Q\xi^{(j)}$. (For the operators we consider, this follows from the rotational invariance of the initial draw $\xi^{(1)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ together with the use of isotropic primitives in subsequent steps; see \cref{rmk:grounding}.)
\end{itemize}
\end{assumption}
%
\begin{remark}[Grounding of assumptions]
\label{rmk:grounding}
Each assumption captures a natural property of practical operators. \textbf{(A1)} holds whenever the operator maintains a running best-so-far and each iteration has a positive probability of improving the incumbent. \textbf{(A2)} reflects diminishing returns: for random search this follows from concavity of the Gaussian order-statistic constant $a_K$ (\citealt{david2003order}, Ch.~4); for $\epsilon$-greedy and local perturbation we verify it explicitly below. \textbf{(A4)} holds for all three operators we analyze, since each uses only isotropic primitives (Gaussian exploration draws and uniform-direction local perturbations); together with the rotational invariance of the initialization $\xi^{(1)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, it implies \textbf{(A3)}: for any unit $u$, pick orthogonal $Q$ with $Qu = e_1$; then $\langle u, \xi^{(j)}\rangle = \langle e_1, Q\xi^{(j)}\rangle$, and by (A4) the process $\{Q\xi^{(j)}\}$ has the same joint law as $\{\xi^{(j)}\}$, so $\max_j \langle u, \xi^{(j)}\rangle$ is distributed identically for every $u$. The factorization $G_t^{\mathcal{L}}(K) = \sigma_t \phi_K^{\mathcal{L}} + O(g_t^2 h)$ is then a \emph{consequence} of (A1)--(A4) plus verifier smoothness (\cref{asm:smooth}), not an assumption: \cref{thm:general-gain} below derives it.
\end{remark}

\subsection{General Gain Factorization}
\label{sec:general-gain}
%
This subsection generalizes the gain decomposition of \cref{thm:loc-scale}\textup{(iii)} from random search to any operator satisfying \cref{asm:local-search}. The result (\cref{thm:general-gain}) shows that the expected gain of any such operator splits into a timestep-dependent sensitivity factor $\sigma_t$ and a $t$-independent operator-specific gain sequence $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$. This separation is what lets the scheduling results in \cref{sec:theory} carry over without change.
%
\begin{theorem}[General gain factorization]
\label{thm:general-gain}
Assume \cref{asm:smooth,asm:local-search} and the iterate moment bound $\mathbb{E}[\max_{1\le j\le K}\|\xi^{(j)}\|^2] \le M_K(\mathcal{L}, d) < \infty$ for some sequence $M_K$ depending on $K$, $d$, and the operator $\mathcal{L}$ (its hyperparameters, e.g., the perturbation radius $\lambda$ for $\epsilon$-greedy/local perturbation).
Then the expected gain from $K$ iterations at timestep $t$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  G_t^{\mathcal{L}}(K) = \sigma_t\,\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}} + O_{K,d,L_t,\mathcal{L}}(g_t^2 h),
  \label{eq:general-gain-factorization}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_t = g_t\sqrt{h}\,\|\nabla\Psi_t(\bar{X}_{t-h};c)\|$ and the implicit constant depends on $K$, $d$, $L_t$, and $M_K(\mathcal{L}, d)$ (hence on the operator hyperparameters), but not on $g_t$ or $\nabla\Psi_t$. The properties of $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$ (strict monotonicity for $K \ge 1$, discrete concavity for $K \ge 1$, $\phi_0^{\mathcal{L}} = 0$) are exactly the assumptions (A1)/(A2) and the convention.
\end{theorem}
%
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:taylor} applied with $\xi = \xi^{(j)}$ for each iterate (the global Hessian bound in \cref{asm:smooth} covers all iterates),
\[
  \Psi_t(\bar{X}_{t-h} + g_t\sqrt{h}\,\xi; c) = \mu_t + \sigma_t \langle u_t, \xi\rangle + R_t(\xi),
\]
where $u_t = \nabla\Psi_t(\bar{X}_{t-h}; c) / \|\nabla\Psi_t(\bar{X}_{t-h}; c)\|$ and $|R_t(\xi)| \le \tfrac{1}{2}L_t g_t^2 h \|\xi\|^2$. Since $\mu_t$ is constant across iterates, substituting into~\cref{eq:gain-general-app} gives
\begin{align*}
  G_t^{\mathcal{L}}(K) &= \mathbb{E}\Bigl[\max_{1 \le j \le K}\bigl(\sigma_t \langle u_t, \xi^{(j)}\rangle + R_t(\xi^{(j)})\bigr)\,\Big|\,X_t, c\Bigr] \\
                       &= \sigma_t\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\max_{1 \le j \le K} \langle u_t, \xi^{(j)}\rangle\,\Big|\,X_t, c\Bigr] + O_{K,d,L_t,\mathcal{L}}(g_t^2 h),
\end{align*}
where the second equality uses the elementary inequality $|\max_j(a_j + b_j) - \max_j a_j| \le \max_j |b_j|$, the non-negativity $\sigma_t \ge 0$ to factor $\sigma_t$ out of the maximum, and the moment-bounded remainder
\[
  \mathbb{E}\bigl[\max_{1 \le j \le K} |R_t(\xi^{(j)})|\bigr] \le \tfrac{1}{2}L_t g_t^2 h\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\max_{1 \le j \le K}\|\xi^{(j)}\|^2\bigr] \le \tfrac{1}{2}L_t g_t^2 h \cdot M_K(\mathcal{L}, d),
\]
where the second inequality is the iterate moment bound posited in the theorem statement.

By (A4), the joint law of $\{\xi^{(j)}\}_{1\le j\le K}$ is invariant under simultaneous rotation, so $\mathbb{E}[\max_{1 \le j \le K} \langle u_t, \xi^{(j)}\rangle]$ does not depend on $u_t$ (this is the property labeled (A3); see \cref{rmk:grounding}). By definition \cref{eq:phi-def}, this expectation is $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$. Strict monotonicity in $K \ge 1$ and discrete concavity of $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$ are the assumptions (A1) and (A2) respectively (verified explicitly for each operator in \cref{sec:operator-gain}); $\phi_0^{\mathcal{L}} = 0$ by convention.
\end{proof}
%
\begin{remark}[Sensitivity-gain separation]
\label{rmk:separation}
\cref{thm:general-gain} establishes the \emph{sensitivity-gain separation}: the timestep-dependent factor $\sigma_t$ and the operator-dependent gain sequence $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$ decouple multiplicatively. Consequently, the global scheduler needs only the per-step sensitivity profile $\{\sigma_t\}_{t=1}^T$, which is common to all operators, and does not require knowledge of the internal workings of $\mathcal{L}$. Conversely, an operator designer can optimize the gain sequence $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$ independently of the scheduling policy. This modularity underpins the two-level design of \textsc{GAINS}.
\end{remark}
%
\subsection{Scheduling Optimality for General Operators}
\label{sec:general-scheduling}
%
This subsection transfers the offline water-filling result and the online Jensen-gap argument from random search to any operator satisfying \cref{asm:local-search}. The proofs in \cref{sec:proof-offline} only invoke three properties of the random-search gain sequence $a_K$: it is increasing in $K$, its marginal increments do not accelerate, and it does not depend on the timestep $t$. \cref{thm:general-gain} guarantees the same three properties for the operator-specific sequence $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$, so we can replace $a_K$ by $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$ throughout the original proofs.
%
\begin{corollary}[Offline water-filling for general operators]
\label{cor:offline-general}
Under the conditions of \cref{thm:general-gain} (including the iterate moment bound) with prompt-independent $\sigma_t$, and assuming the $O_{K,d,L_t,\mathcal{L}}(g_t^2 h)$ remainder is uniform in $(c, x_t)$ (this is automatic for i.i.d.\ random search; for non-i.i.d.\ operators such as $\epsilon$-greedy or local perturbation it amounts to bounding $M_K(\mathcal{L}, d)$ uniformly across initializations), the allocation problem
\begin{equation}
  \max_{\{K_t\}:\,\sum_t K_t = B,\;K_t \ge 1}\;\sum_{t=1}^{T} \sigma_t\,\phi_{K_t}^{\mathcal{L}}
  \label{eq:alloc-general}
\end{equation}
has the same structure as \cref{thm:offline}:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textup{(i)}] The optimal allocation $\{K_t^*\}$ is simultaneously optimal for every instance.
\item[\textup{(ii)}] \emph{Weak monotonicity}: $\sigma_{t_1} > \sigma_{t_2}$ implies $K_{t_1}^* \ge K_{t_2}^*$. Strictness at the integer level requires the $\sigma$-gap to exceed a discrete-marginal step (cf.\ \cref{thm:offline}\textup{(ii)}); the continuous relaxation gives strict monotonicity unconditionally when $\phi_K$ is strictly concave. When $\phi_K$ is linear, every optimizer concentrates the allocable budget on $\arg\max_t \sigma_t$ (broken by ties when this set is non-singleton).
\item[\textup{(iii)}] Non-uniform allocation weakly dominates uniform; the dominance is strict whenever $\{\sigma_t\}$ are not all equal and either $\phi_K$ is strictly concave (in the continuous relaxation) or $\phi_K$ is affine and $B > T$ (the corner solution differs from the uniform interior).
\end{itemize}
\end{corollary}
%
\begin{proof}
The proof splits by curvature. Because \cref{thm:general-gain} gives a common, $t$-independent gain sequence $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$ with non-accelerating marginals, the discrete exchange argument from \cref{thm:offline} still shows that an optimal allocation can be chosen monotone in $\sigma_t$ and that moving budget from a lower-$\sigma_t$ timestep to a higher-$\sigma_t$ timestep never hurts, establishing parts (i) and (iii) at the weak level.

When $\phi_K$ is strictly concave, the KKT argument from \cref{sec:proof-offline} applies verbatim with $\phi_K$ in place of $a_K$, yielding the strict water-filling ordering among active timesteps. When $\phi_K$ is affine, say $\phi_K = \alpha(K - \beta)$ with $\alpha > 0$ (e.g., local perturbation has $\alpha = c_d(\lambda)$, $\beta = 1$), \cref{eq:alloc-general} becomes $\alpha \sum_t \sigma_t K_t - \alpha\beta\sum_t \sigma_t$; the additive constant $-\alpha\beta\sum_t\sigma_t$ does not depend on the allocation, so the problem reduces to a linear program $\alpha \sum_t \sigma_t K_t$, and every optimizer assigns all budget beyond the mandatory floor $K_t \ge 1$ to $\arg\max_t \sigma_t$.
\end{proof}
%
\begin{remark}[Curvature dichotomy in optimal scheduling]
\label{rmk:curvature}
The structure of the optimal allocation depends qualitatively on the curvature of $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$. When $\phi_K$ is strictly concave (e.g., random search, where $\phi_K = a_K \sim \sqrt{2\log K}$), the optimal schedule distributes budget smoothly across timesteps via water-filling. When $\phi_K$ is affine in $K$ (e.g., local perturbation in the linearized regime, where $\phi_K^{(\mathrm{LP})} = c_d(\lambda)\,(K-1)$), every optimizer concentrates the allocable budget on $\arg\max_t \sigma_t$. The choice of local search operator therefore influences not only the per-step gain rate but also the \emph{shape} of the optimal global schedule (cf.\ \cref{tab:phi-summary}).
\end{remark}
%
\begin{corollary}[Online Jensen gap and dual-threshold stopping]
\label{cor:online-general}
Under the conditions of \cref{thm:general-gain} with prompt-dependent $\sigma_t = \sigma_t(c, x_t)$, define the generalized oracle
\begin{equation}
  V_{\mathcal{L}}^*(\boldsymbol{\sigma}) = \max_{\{K_t\}:\,\sum_t K_t = B,\;K_t \ge 1}\;\sum_{t=1}^{T} \sigma_t\,\phi_{K_t}^{\mathcal{L}}.
  \label{eq:oracle-general}
\end{equation}
Then $V_{\mathcal{L}}^*$ is convex in $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, and
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\bigl[V_{\mathcal{L}}^*(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\bigr] \ge V_{\mathcal{L}}^*\bigl(\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\bigr),
  \label{eq:jensen-general}
\end{equation}
with equality whenever the same allocation is optimal almost surely on the support of $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$. The one-step marginal gain satisfies, for integer budgets,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_t^{\mathcal{L}}(K) := G_t^{\mathcal{L}}(K{+}1) - G_t^{\mathcal{L}}(K) = \sigma_t \cdot \Delta\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}} + O_{K,d,L_t,\mathcal{L}}(g_t^2 h),
  \qquad \Delta\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}} := \phi_{K+1}^{\mathcal{L}} - \phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}.
  \label{eq:marginal-general}
\end{equation}
The dual-threshold stopping rule of \cref{alg:gains} applies as a heuristic proxy control law. For random search the within-step empirical variance $\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}\{\hat s_t^{(j)}\}$ consistently estimates $\sigma_t^2$ (via \cref{thm:loc-scale}\textup{(ii)}, since the candidates are i.i.d.); for non-i.i.d.\ operators (e.g., $\epsilon$-greedy, local perturbation) this estimator is biased because exploit candidates are correlated with the running incumbent, so $\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}\{\hat s_t^{(j)}\}$ should be read as a bias-tolerant sensitivity proxy rather than an unbiased estimate of $\sigma_t^2$. The empirical effectiveness of this proxy across operators is documented in \cref{sec:appendix-operator-compat}.
\end{corollary}
%
\begin{proof}
For convexity: for each fixed allocation $\{K_t\}$, the map $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \mapsto \sum_t \sigma_t\,\phi_{K_t}^{\mathcal{L}}$ is linear. $V_{\mathcal{L}}^*$ is the pointwise maximum of finitely many linear functions, hence convex. Jensen's inequality gives \cref{eq:jensen-general}. This argument uses no property of $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$ beyond it being a real-valued constant for each $K$.

For the marginal gain: the factorization \cref{eq:marginal-general} follows by taking first differences in \cref{eq:general-gain-factorization}. The dual-threshold logic relies on the product structure of the marginal gain and the non-accelerating property of $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$, both of which persist under (A1)--(A4). The location-scale variance proxy from \cref{thm:loc-scale}\textup{(ii)} is exact for i.i.d.\ random search and serves as a bias-tolerant heuristic sensitivity proxy for non-i.i.d.\ operators (as noted in the corollary statement).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Operator-Specific Gain Sequences}
\label{sec:operator-gain}
%
This subsection instantiates the abstract framework on three concrete local operators: random search, $\epsilon$-greedy search, and local perturbation search (the $\epsilon=0$ limit of $\epsilon$-greedy). For each operator we derive the gain sequence $\phi_K^{\mathcal{L}}$ in closed form (or as a tight lower bound) and verify the four assumptions (A1)--(A4). All three derivations use the linearized regime $g_t\sqrt{h} \to 0$ in which the verifier reduces to a linear function of the noise, $\Psi_t(\bar{X}_{t-h} + g_t\sqrt{h}\,\xi; c) = \mu_t + \sigma_t \langle u, \xi\rangle + O(g_t^2 h)$ (\cref{prop:taylor}); the closed-form sequences should therefore be read as the leading-order behavior in this regime.
%
\begin{example}[Random search]
\label{ex:random-search}
The operator draws $K$ i.i.d.\ $\xi_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and keeps the best. In the linearized regime, the projected scores $\langle u, \xi_k\rangle$ are i.i.d.\ $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, so
\begin{equation}
  \phi_K^{\mathrm{RS}} = a_K := \mathbb{E}\bigl[\max(Z_1, \ldots, Z_K)\bigr],
  \qquad Z_k \stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, 1).
  \label{eq:phi-rs}
\end{equation}
\textbf{(A1):} For $K \ge 1$, a fresh draw has positive probability of exceeding the current max, so $\phi_K^{\mathrm{RS}} = a_K$ is strictly increasing in $K$. \textbf{(A2):} For $K \ge 1$, write $\Delta_K = \mathbb{E}[h(M_K)]$ with $h(m) := \mathbb{E}[(Z-m)_+]$ and $M_K := \max(Z_1, \ldots, Z_K)$. Since $h$ is strictly decreasing on $\mathbb{R}$ (it is differentiable with $h'(m) = -(1-\Phi(m)) < 0$) and $M_{K+1} = \max(M_K, Z_{K+1}) \ge M_K$ a.s.\ with strict inequality on the positive-probability event $\{Z_{K+1} > M_K\}$, we have $h(M_{K+1}) \le h(M_K)$ a.s.\ with strict inequality on the same event. Taking expectations gives $\Delta_{K+1} < \Delta_K$ for all $K \ge 1$ (this is the standard concavity of $a_K$ for $K \ge 1$; see \citealt{david2003order}, Ch.~4). \textbf{(A3):} By \cref{thm:loc-scale}\textup{(iii)}, $G_t(K) = \sigma_t\,a_K + O_{K,d,L_t}(g_t^2 h)$; the gain sequence $a_K \sim \sqrt{2\log K}$ is $t$-independent. \textbf{(A4):} i.i.d.\ $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ draws are rotationally invariant.
\end{example}
%
\begin{example}[$\epsilon$-greedy search]
\label{ex:eps-greedy}
The first iterate is drawn $\xi^{(1)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ as the initial incumbent. For $j \ge 2$, with probability $\epsilon$ the operator explores by drawing $\xi^{(j)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$; otherwise it exploits by setting $\xi^{(j)} = \xi^* + RU$ (perturbing the current best $\xi^*$), where $U$ is uniform on the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ and $R \sim \mathrm{Unif}[0, \lambda\sqrt{d}]$, and updates $\xi^*$ if the new candidate is better. In the linearized regime, the verifier satisfies $\Psi_t = \mu_t + \sigma_t \langle u, \xi\rangle + O(g_t^2 h)$ (\cref{prop:taylor}), so the operator's accept/reject test on $\Psi_t$ coincides with the projected test on $\langle u, \xi\rangle$ to leading order; in particular, the incumbent $\xi^*$ also realizes the projected best-so-far $\langle u, \xi^*\rangle = \max_{j \le K}\langle u, \xi^{(j)}\rangle$. Then:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Exploration steps} (random count $N+1 \sim 1+\mathrm{Bin}(K-1,\epsilon)$): each is an i.i.d.\ draw from $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ in the projected coordinate $\langle u, \xi\rangle$, contributing a gain of $\mathbb{E}[a_{N+1}]$.
  \item \emph{Exploitation steps} (expected count $(1{-}\epsilon)(K{-}1)$, since $\xi^{(1)}$ is always an exploration draw): each perturbs the current best in the projected direction by $R\langle u, U\rangle$. The accepted increment is $(R\langle u, U\rangle)_+$, whose expectation is the uniform-ball drift constant
  \begin{equation}
    c_d(\lambda) := \mathbb{E}\bigl[(R\langle u, U\rangle)_+\bigr] = \frac{\lambda\sqrt{d}}{4\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{\Gamma(d/2)}{\Gamma((d+1)/2)}.
    \label{eq:uniform-ball-drift}
  \end{equation}
\end{itemize}
Thus exploitation adds an affine-in-$K$ drift beyond the exploration component. Let $N \sim \mathrm{Bin}(K-1, \epsilon)$ count the number of exploration steps among $\xi^{(2)},\ldots,\xi^{(K)}$ (the initial $\xi^{(1)}\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ is always an exploration draw). Conditional on $N$, the exploration contribution to the projected best is $a_{N+1}$ in expectation, so we retain the lower bound
\begin{equation}
  \phi_K^{(\epsilon)} \ge \mathbb{E}\bigl[a_{N+1}\bigr], \qquad N \sim \mathrm{Bin}(K-1, \epsilon),
  \label{eq:phi-epsgreedy-lb}
\end{equation}
with equality when exploitation steps add no improvement (e.g., $\lambda \to 0$). When $\epsilon = 1$, $N = K-1$ deterministically and the operator reduces to random search with $\phi_K^{(1)} = a_K$.

\textbf{(A1):} For $K \ge 1$, each subsequent iteration has probability $\epsilon > 0$ of being an exploration step that draws a fresh $Z_{\mathrm{new}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ in the projected coordinate. Because $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ has unbounded support, $P(Z_{\mathrm{new}} > \max_{1 \le j \le K} \langle u, \xi^{(j)}\rangle) > 0$, so $\phi_{K+1}^{(\epsilon)} > \phi_K^{(\epsilon)}$ for all $K \ge 1$ (matching the $K \ge 1$ scope of (A1); at $K = 0$, $\phi_1 = \phi_0 = 0$ since $\xi^{(1)}$ has zero-mean projection).

\textbf{(A2):} For $K \ge 1$, let $M_K = \max_{1 \le j \le K} \langle u, \xi^{(j)}\rangle$ and $h(m) := \mathbb{E}[(Z-m)_+]$ for $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ (strictly decreasing). The marginal gain decomposes as
\[
  \Delta_K = \underbrace{\epsilon \cdot \mathbb{E}[h(M_K)]}_{\text{explore}} + \underbrace{(1-\epsilon)\,c_d(\lambda)}_{\text{exploit}},
\]
since on an exploration step $\langle u, \xi^{(K+1)}\rangle \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ is independent of $M_K$, and on an exploitation step the projected increment $(R\langle u, U\rangle)_+$ is independent of $M_K$ with expectation $c_d(\lambda)$. The exploit term is constant in $K$. For the explore term: $M_{K+1} \ge M_K$ \emph{a.s.}\ (running max under either step type), so $h(M_{K+1}) \le h(M_K)$ a.s.; on the positive-probability event $\{M_{K+1} > M_K\}$ (which occurs whenever step $K+1$ improves the incumbent, e.g., probability $\epsilon \cdot \Pr(Z > M_K) + (1-\epsilon) \cdot \frac12 > 0$), the inequality is strict. Taking expectations: $\mathbb{E}[h(M_{K+1})] < \mathbb{E}[h(M_K)]$ for all $K \ge 1$. Hence $\Delta_{K+1} - \Delta_K = \epsilon \cdot (\mathbb{E}[h(M_{K+1})] - \mathbb{E}[h(M_K)]) < 0$ when $\epsilon > 0$ (and equals $0$ when $\epsilon = 0$, which reduces to \cref{ex:local-perturbation}); (A2) holds for $K \ge 1$, strictly when $\epsilon > 0$.

\textbf{(A3):} In the linearized regime, both the exploration draw $\langle u, \xi_{\mathrm{new}}\rangle \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ and the exploitation perturbation $R\langle u, U\rangle$ depend on $u$ only through a one-dimensional projection, whose distribution is $u$-independent by isotropy. Hence $\phi_K^{(\epsilon)}$ does not depend on the timestep $t$.

\textbf{(A4):} Both primitives (Gaussian draws for explore, sphere-uniform directions with scalar radii for exploit) are rotationally invariant; the accept-reject comparison uses $\langle u, \xi\rangle$, which satisfies $\langle Qu, Q\xi\rangle = \langle u, \xi\rangle$ for any orthogonal $Q$.
\end{example}
%
\begin{example}[Local perturbation search ($\epsilon$-greedy with $\epsilon = 0$)]
\label{ex:local-perturbation}
This operator is the pure-exploitation limit of \cref{ex:eps-greedy}: each iteration perturbs the current best $\xi^*$ by $RU$ with $U \sim \mathrm{Unif}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})$ and $R \sim \mathrm{Unif}[0, \lambda\sqrt{d}]$, setting $\xi_{\mathrm{new}} = \xi^* + RU$, and updates $\xi^*$ if the new candidate scores higher. In the linearized regime, the projected coordinate of the incumbent (which equals the projected best-so-far, since the verifier is linear in $\xi$ to leading order) evolves by accept-reject: the perturbation $R\langle u, U\rangle$ is accepted when $\langle u, \xi_{\mathrm{new}}\rangle > \langle u, \xi^*\rangle$, i.e., when $\langle u, U\rangle > 0$. Because the acceptance event is independent of the current incumbent, each iteration $j \ge 2$ adds a constant expected improvement, giving the marginal recursion
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{K+1}^{(\mathrm{LP})} - \phi_K^{(\mathrm{LP})} = c_d(\lambda) \quad (K \ge 1),
  \label{eq:phi-lp-marginal}
\end{equation}
and, combined with the convention $\phi_0^{(\mathrm{LP})} = \phi_1^{(\mathrm{LP})} = 0$ (the initial iterate $\xi^{(1)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ has zero-mean projection),
\begin{equation}
  \phi_K^{(\mathrm{LP})} = c_d(\lambda)\,(K-1) \quad (K \ge 1).
  \label{eq:phi-lp-exact}
\end{equation}
\textbf{(A1):} For $\lambda > 0$ and $d \ge 1$ (so $c_d(\lambda) > 0$), each perturbation has probability $P(\langle u, U\rangle > 0) = 1/2 > 0$ of improving the incumbent, so $\phi_K$ is strictly increasing in $K$. \textbf{(A2):} The marginal gain \cref{eq:phi-lp-marginal} is the constant $c_d(\lambda)$ for all $K \ge 1$, so $\Delta_{K+1} = \Delta_K$ trivially satisfies the non-increasing condition (with equality). The resulting $\phi_K^{(\mathrm{LP})} = c_d(\lambda)\,(K-1)$ is affine in $K$ (weakly concave but not strictly), which is why \cref{cor:offline-general}\textup{(ii)} treats this case separately: under affine $\phi_K$, every optimizer concentrates allocable budget on $\arg\max_t \sigma_t$ rather than spreading it via water-filling. Outside the linearized regime, curvature effects perturb this exact constant-marginal picture; the abstract theory uses \cref{eq:phi-lp-exact} only as the leading-order gain sequence. \textbf{(A3):} The perturbation $R\langle u, U\rangle$ is a one-dimensional projection of a uniform-ball perturbation, whose distribution does not depend on $u$ or $t$. Hence $\phi_K^{(\mathrm{LP})}$ is $t$-independent. \textbf{(A4):} Same as the $\epsilon$-greedy case.
\end{example}

\label{tab:phi-summary}
\small

\cref{tab:phi-summary} shows that the three operators differ qualitatively in growth rate. Random search saturates logarithmically, while local perturbation grows linearly in the linearized regime. This implies that the optimal operator choice depends on the per-step budget: smaller budgets are well served by random search, while at larger budgets the linear-growth local perturbation operator becomes relatively more attractive. A precise crossover analysis would require characterizing how the higher-order remainder $R_t(\xi) = O(g_t^2 h \|\xi\|^2)$ couples to the operator-dependent iterate norms outside the linearized regime, where the iterates of local perturbation may accumulate drift; we leave this to future work and treat the within-regime ordering as a guideline rather than a theorem.

\section{Additional Experiments and Detailed Analysis}
\label{sec:appendix-experiments}

This appendix collects four supporting experiments referenced in \cref{sec:experiments} and reads each one against the theory. The four are: (i) an ablation that separates the offline allocation stage of \textsc{GAINS} from its online control stage (\cref{sec:appendix-ablation}); (ii) a generalization study on a larger and more diverse prompt set (\cref{sec:appendix-prompt-set}); (iii) a compatibility test that swaps the local search operator while keeping the global scheduler fixed (\cref{sec:appendix-operator-compat}); and (iv) an evaluation on flow-based models, where stochasticity is reduced compared to standard diffusion (\cref{sec:appendix-flow}). \subsection{Ablation: Offline vs.\ Offline+Online}
\label{sec:appendix-ablation}
%
This subsection isolates the two scheduling stages of \textsc{GAINS} (offline allocation across timesteps and online per-prompt control) and measures how much each stage contributes. \cref{tab:sd_400_ablation} reports Stable Diffusion results at a fixed total budget of $400$ NFE. The uniform-allocation benchmark achieves Brightness $0.7025$ and Compressibility $0.8833$. Replacing the uniform allocation with the offline schedule alone (the per-timestep budgets $\{K_t\}$ are fixed in advance, and online early stopping is disabled) already lifts both metrics by $+0.0133$ on Brightness and $+0.0040$ on Compressibility, indicating that even coarse per-timestep budgeting captures genuine model-level structure that does not require any per-prompt adjustment. Adding online feedback on top (the full \textsc{GAINS}) yields the highest scores, $0.7248$ Brightness and $0.8946$ Compressibility, an incremental $+0.0090$ on Brightness and $+0.0073$ on Compressibility beyond the offline-only setting.

The incremental gain from online over offline-only ($+0.0090$ Brightness, $+0.0073$ Compressibility) is the empirical Jensen gap $\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}[V^*(\boldsymbol{\sigma})] - V^*(\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})$: because per-prompt sensitivities vary, a single allocation tuned to the mean profile leaves value on the table, and the online controller recovers part of that gap.

\subsection{Flow-Based Models: Reduced Stochasticity}
\label{sec:appendix-flow}
%
This subsection extends the evaluation to flow-based generative models, where the per-step transition is deterministic and so contains no noise variable to search over. Following \citet{dockhorn2024stochastic} and \citet{kim2025rbf}, we inject Gaussian stochasticity at a small set of selected timesteps, converting the original ordinary differential equation (ODE) sampler into a stochastic differential equation (SDE) sampler with matching marginal distributions. Once written in the per-step form of \cref{eq:transition}, the resulting sampler admits the same noise-trajectory search as standard diffusion, and \textsc{GAINS} applies without modifying the pretrained flow model.

Even so, the quality curve shifts to the left at every budget, and \textsc{GAINS} also reduces the run-to-run standard deviations (for example, from $0.0058$ to $0.0046$ for Compressibility at 150 NFE), indicating that even modest reallocation tightens the per-prompt distribution of outcomes.
