\section{The \textsc{GAINS} Algorithm and Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:algorithm}

We now develop \textbf{\textsc{GAINS}} (\textbf{G}lobal \textbf{A}daptive \textbf{I}nference-time \textbf{N}oise \textbf{S}cheduling). The algorithm operates at two granularities: offline coarse allocation (\cref{sec:offline}) sets per-timestep budgets from a small calibration run, and online control (\cref{sec:online}) adapts those budgets per prompt. The theoretical analysis (\cref{sec:theory}) treats the special case where the local operator is pure random search and shows that the gain decomposes into a per-step sensitivity factor and a sample-size effect, yielding a water-filling allocation. We then prove an $\Omega(T)$ regret lower bound for adaptive policies and a matching $O(g_t^2 h\cdot T)$ upper bound for \textsc{GAINS}. Extensions to general operators ($\epsilon$-greedy, local perturbation) appear in \cref{sec:appendix-general}.

\subsection{Offline Coarse Time Scheduling}
\label{sec:offline}

We consider the common setting where the sampler maintains a single latent and a single noise $(x_t, \varepsilon_t)$ at each timestep but performs multiple local search iterations before committing to $x_{t-1}$. The global scheduling problem then reduces to per-timestep decisions: at each $t$, given the search history and remaining budget, should we continue local search or stop and move on?

For each model and dataset, we run \emph{offline} profiling on a small set of prompts: we execute noise search with a uniform iteration count at every timestep and log verifier scores after each iteration. From these logs we compute, for each timestep $t$, the average score gain and its variability across examples. Aggregating reveals which segments of the trajectory respond most to noise refinement: for Stable Diffusion, gains concentrate on early timesteps; for EDM they cluster in a contiguous middle segment. We partition timesteps into a high-value region and a low-value region, assign more NFE to the high-value region, and derive a coarse per-step budget $\{K_t\}$.

\subsection{Online Fine-Grained Scheduling}
\label{sec:online}

At test time, an online controller refines how the pre-assigned budget $\{K_t\}$ is spent on each prompt. At timestep $t$, local search runs up to $K_t$ iterations, but the controller can stop early or, within a small window, modulate spending. It monitors two statistics: the \emph{score gain} of local search and the \emph{score variance} across candidates. Letting $s_t^{(j)}$ be the best score after $j$ iterations and $\{s_{t,\text{cand}}^{(j,i)}\}_i$ the candidate scores in iteration $j$, define
\begin{equation}
  g_t^{(j)} = s_t^{(j)} - s_t^{(j-1)},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{Var}_t^{(j)} = \mathrm{Var}_i\bigl(s_{t,\text{cand}}^{(j,i)}\bigr).
\end{equation}
$g_t^{(j)}$ measures whether search still finds better noises, while $\mathrm{Var}_t^{(j)}$ reflects the verifier's sensitivity to noise changes. The controller stops early when both recent gains are small \emph{and} candidate variance is small; otherwise it continues. A \emph{revert-on-negative} rule keeps the previous best whenever an iteration produces $g_t^{(j)}<0$, ensuring monotone score within each step. To match realized compute exactly to the budget, over-/under-spending in the high-value region is offset against low-value steps. \cref{alg:gains} gives the procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\textsc{GAINS}: Global Adaptive Inference-time Noise Scheduling}
\label{alg:gains}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} timesteps $\{t_T,\dots,t_1\}$, total budget $B$, offline allocation $\{K_t\}$ ($\sum_t K_t=B$), slack $\delta$, gain window $W_g$, threshold coefs $(\beta_g,\beta_\sigma)$, black-box $\textsc{LocalIter}(t)$.
\STATE \textbf{Output:} realized per-step iterations $\{\widehat K_t\}$.
\STATE $R \leftarrow B$;\ \ $\mathcal{H}_g, \mathcal{H}_\sigma\leftarrow\emptyset$ (history).
\FOR{$t=T,T\!-\!1,\dots,1$}
    \STATE $K \leftarrow \min(K_t, R)$;\ $K_{\max}\leftarrow \min(K+\delta, R)$;\ $j_{\text{watch}}\leftarrow \max(1, K-\delta)$;\ $s^{(0)} \leftarrow -\infty$;\ $\mathcal{H}\leftarrow\emptyset$;\ $\widehat K_t \leftarrow 0$.
    \FOR{$j=1,\dots,K_{\max}$}
        \STATE $(s^{(j)}, \sigma^{(j)}) \leftarrow \textsc{LocalIter}(t)$
        \IF{$s^{(j)} < s^{(j-1)}$} \STATE $s^{(j)} \leftarrow s^{(j-1)}$ \hfill {\small\COMMENT{revert-on-negative}} \ENDIF
        \STATE push $g^{(j)}\!\leftarrow\!s^{(j)}\!-\!s^{(j-1)}$ into $\mathcal{H}$ (keep last $W_g$);\ $\widetilde g^{(j)}\!\leftarrow\!\mathrm{Mean}(\mathcal{H})$;\ $\widehat K_t\!\leftarrow\!\widehat K_t+1$;\ $R \leftarrow R-1$.
        \IF{$R=0$} \STATE \textbf{break} \ENDIF
        \IF{$j \ge j_{\text{watch}}$ \AND $|\mathcal{H}_g|>0$ \AND $|\mathcal{H}_\sigma|>0$ \AND $\widetilde g^{(j)} < \beta_g \mathrm{Mean}(\mathcal{H}_g)$ \AND $\sigma^{(j)} < \beta_\sigma \mathrm{Mean}(\mathcal{H}_\sigma)$}
            \STATE \textbf{break}
        \ENDIF
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE append $\mathrm{Mean}(\mathcal{H})$ to $\mathcal{H}_g$;\ append $\sigma^{(\widehat K_t)}$ to $\mathcal{H}_\sigma$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{return} $\{\widehat K_t\}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Theoretical Foundations}
\label{sec:theory}

Both stages of \textsc{GAINS} rely on the per-step score distribution $\mathcal{D}_t$ exhibiting location-scale structure under the diffusion SDE. In Euler--Maruyama form with step size $h$ and diffusion coefficient $g_t$, the backward denoising process (\cref{eq:ddpm-reverse}) becomes $X_{t-h} = X_t + b_t(X_t,c)\,h + g_t\sqrt{h}\,\xi$ with $\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$. Define the verifier score function $\Psi_t(x;c) := v(D_\theta(x, t{-}h, c);\,c)$, the score from one noise candidate $S_t := \Psi_t(X_{t-h};c)$, and the deterministic Euler point $\bar{X}_{t-h} := X_t + b_t(X_t,c)\,h$.

\begin{assumption}[Verifier smoothness]
\label{asm:smooth}
$\Psi_t(\cdot\,;c)\in C^2(\mathbb{R}^d)$, and there exists $L_t<\infty$ such that $\|\nabla^2\Psi_t(x;c)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le L_t$ for all $x$ in a neighborhood of the segment between $\bar{X}_{t-h}$ and $X_{t-h}$, almost surely.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Location-scale structure of per-step scores]
\label{thm:loc-scale}
Under \cref{asm:smooth}, define $\mu_t := \Psi_t(\bar{X}_{t-h};c)$ and $\sigma_t := g_t\sqrt{h}\,\|\nabla\Psi_t(\bar{X}_{t-h};c)\|$. Conditional on $(X_t,c)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] there exists $Z_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ such that $S_t = \mu_t + \sigma_t Z_t + R_t$ with $|R_t|\le\tfrac{1}{2}L_t g_t^2 h\|\xi\|^2$ a.s.;
\item[\textup{(ii)}] as $g_t\sqrt{h}\to 0$, $\mathrm{Var}(S_t\mid X_t,c) = \sigma_t^2 + O_d(g_t^3 h^{3/2})$;
\item[\textup{(iii)}] for $K$ i.i.d.\ copies $S_t^{(1)},\ldots,S_t^{(K)}$,
\begin{equation}
G_t(K) := \mathbb{E}\Bigl[\max_{1\le k\le K} S_t^{(k)}\,\Big|\,X_t,c\Bigr] - \mu_t = \sigma_t\,a_K + O_{K,d,L_t}(g_t^2 h),
\label{eq:gain-sde}
\end{equation}
where $a_K := \mathbb{E}[\max(Z_1,\ldots,Z_K)]$ with $Z_k\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,1)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proof (in \cref{sec:proof-loc-scale}) is a second-order Taylor expansion around $\bar{X}_{t-h}$: the score decomposes into a deterministic baseline, a Gaussian linear term with variance $\sigma_t^2$, and a higher-order remainder. \cref{thm:loc-scale} identifies the precise quantities governing per-step sensitivity through the product $\sigma_t = g_t\sqrt{h}\,\|\nabla\Psi_t\|$ of diffusion coefficient, step size, and verifier gradient norm.

\paragraph{Optimal allocation: water-filling.}
Because the gain factorizes as $G_t(K) = \sigma_t a_K + O(g_t^2 h)$, global scheduling reduces, up to controlled remainders, to
\begin{equation}
  \max_{K_1,\dots,K_T}\;\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sigma_t\,a_{K_t}
  \quad\text{s.t.}\;\sum_{t=1}^{T}K_t=B,\;K_t\ge 1.
  \label{eq:alloc}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Offline optimality under independence]
\label{thm:offline}
Suppose $\sigma_t$ depends only on $t$, not on prompt or search history. Then:
\textup{(i)} \cref{eq:alloc} decomposes across prompts: any optimal $\{K_t^*\}$ is simultaneously optimal for every instance.
\textup{(ii)} The optimal allocation equalizes marginal gains: $\sigma_t\,\Delta a_{K_t^*} = \lambda^*$ for all $t$ with $K_t^*>1$, where $\Delta a_K := a_{K+1}-a_K$ and $\lambda^*$ is the shadow price; consequently, $K_t^*$ is increasing in $\sigma_t$ (water-filling).
\textup{(iii)} $\sum_t \sigma_t a_{K_t^*} > \sum_t \sigma_t a_{B/T}$ whenever $\{\sigma_t\}$ are not all equal, and the gap grows with the dispersion of $\{\sigma_t\}$.
\end{theorem}

The proof (\cref{sec:proof-offline}) uses Lagrangian first-order conditions and concavity of $a_K$.

\paragraph{Regret bounds for adaptive scheduling.}
When $\sigma_t = \sigma_t(c, x_t)$ varies with the prompt, the offline allocation is no longer optimal per-instance; an adaptive policy must trade off committing to a step versus saving budget for later. We bound the regret of adaptive policies against the offline-optimal benchmark.

\begin{lemma}[Regret lower bound]
\label{lem:lower}
Consider sequential budget allocation over $T$ rounds with total budget $B$. At round $t$, the learner observes only the current distribution $\mathcal{D}_t$, chooses $K_t\in\{0,1\}$ subject to $\sum_t K_t \le B$, and receives reward drawn from $\mathcal{D}_t$ (or zero if $K_t=0$). Let regret be measured against the offline benchmark that knows $\mathcal{D}_1,\ldots,\mathcal{D}_T$ in advance. Then for every adaptive policy there exists an instance with $T = 2B$ such that
\begin{equation}
R_T \;\ge\; \Omega(T).
\label{eq:lower}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

The construction (\cref{sec:proof-lower}) places half the rounds at deterministic reward $1/2$ and the remaining half at one of two extremes (all zeros vs.\ all ones); without future information, any allocation incurs $\Omega(T)$ regret on at least one instance. This establishes that linear regret is unavoidable in the worst case.

We now show that \textsc{GAINS} attains a matching upper bound up to leading-order remainders, at the same order as the linearization error of the SDE.

\begin{theorem}[Regret upper bound for \textsc{GAINS}]
\label{thm:regret-upper}
Let $V_{\mathrm{MDP}}^*$ denote the value of the Bellman-optimal policy for the sequential allocation MDP (\cref{sec:mdp}) and $V_{\textsc{GAINS}}$ the value achieved by \textsc{GAINS}. Under \cref{asm:smooth} and the location-scale model, in the linearized regime $g_t\sqrt{h}\to 0$,
\begin{equation}
  V_{\mathrm{MDP}}^* - V_{\textsc{GAINS}} \;=\; O(g_t^2 h\cdot T),
\label{eq:regret-upper}
\end{equation}
matching the lower bound \cref{eq:lower} up to the SDE linearization scale.
\end{theorem}

The proof (\cref{sec:proof-regret-upper}) decomposes the regret into a \emph{dynamic gap} (path-dependence between MDP and static oracle), a \emph{profiling error} (using $\hat{\boldsymbol\sigma}$ in place of $\boldsymbol\sigma$), and an \emph{online adjustment} term, and bounds each at $O(g_t^2 h)$ per step using the location-scale factorization.

\begin{remark}[Online controller as Jensen-gap recovery]
\label{rmk:online-value}
When $\sigma_t = \sigma_t(c, x_t)$, the static oracle $V^*(\boldsymbol\sigma) = \max_{\sum_t K_t = B}\sum_t \sigma_t a_{K_t}$ is convex in $\boldsymbol\sigma$ as a pointwise maximum of linear functions; hence $\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol\sigma}[V^*(\boldsymbol\sigma)] - V^*(\bar{\boldsymbol\sigma}) \ge 0$ by Jensen. The online controller targets this Jensen gap: low gain with high candidate variance signals a responsive but exhausted step (premature to stop), while moderate gain with low variance signals inherent insensitivity (search remains productive). \cref{fig:stopping} shows the resulting two-threshold stopping rule. A second-order characterization of the gap appears in \cref{sec:appendix-general}.
\end{remark}
