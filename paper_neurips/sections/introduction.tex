\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Diffusion models generate data by following a \emph{denoising trajectory}: starting from random noise, the model applies a sequence of learned noise-reduction steps until a sample from the target distribution emerges~\citep{song2019generative,ho2020denoising,tang2024sde}. Each sample requires $T$ sequential denoising steps, and each step costs one forward pass through the neural network, which we call a \emph{function evaluation} (NFE). Recent work shows that spending more than the standard $T$ NFE per sample can improve sample quality without retraining the model~\citep{ma2024inference,ramesh2025nts}, an idea known as \emph{inference-time scaling}. This raises a resource-allocation question: how should the extra NFE be distributed across the $T$ denoising steps?

Two strategies have been studied. The first \emph{lengthens the trajectory} by increasing $T$, which yields quickly diminishing returns. The second \emph{searches within each timestep} by evaluating multiple noise candidates and keeping the one that scores highest on a quality metric (a \emph{verifier}, such as an image-quality scorer). Existing methods instantiate the second strategy with various local operators: $\epsilon$-greedy exploration~\citep{ramesh2025nts}, zero-order noise optimization~\citep{tang2024dno}, and particle-based refinement~\citep{kim2025rbf}.

Most existing methods, however, distribute the search budget uniformly across denoising steps or hard-code it to a fixed window, ignoring that replacing a noise draw with a better candidate improves quality far more at some steps than at others. Refining noise at steps with high sensitivity yields large quality improvements, while the same computation at steps with low sensitivity has negligible effect. This leaves a fundamental question unanswered: given a fixed total NFE budget, \emph{where along the trajectory} should search concentrate?

\textbf{Contributions.} This is a computational budget-allocation problem: given $B$ total NFE across $T$ sequential denoising steps, find the allocation maximizing output quality. We formalize the question as \emph{noise trajectory search} and decompose it into two levels: \emph{local} decisions about how to search at a given timestep, and \emph{global} decisions about how to distribute the budget across the trajectory. While prior work has focused on increasingly sophisticated local operators, principled global allocation remains largely open.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Two-level framework.} We introduce a unified two-level view of noise trajectory search that separates local noise refinement from global timestep scheduling, covering both stochastic and deterministic samplers and unifying existing inference-time methods as special-case scheduling policies (\cref{sec:framework}).
  \item \textbf{The \textsc{GAINS} algorithm.} We develop \textbf{\textsc{GAINS}} (\textbf{G}lobal \textbf{A}daptive \textbf{I}nference-time \textbf{N}oise \textbf{S}cheduling), an algorithm that integrates offline timestep profiling with online feedback control, enforcing an exact NFE budget (\cref{sec:algorithm}).
  \item \textbf{Theoretical foundations.} A second-order Taylor expansion of the verifier score function shows that the per-step score distribution is approximately location-scale with sensitivity $\sigma_t = g_t\sqrt{h}\,\|\nabla\Psi_t\|$ (\cref{thm:loc-scale}). Optimal offline budgeting follows a water-filling structure (\cref{thm:offline}); for adaptive policies we prove an $\Omega(T)$ regret lower bound (\cref{lem:lower}) and a matching $O(g_t^2 h\cdot T)$ upper bound for \textsc{GAINS} (\cref{thm:regret-upper}).
  \item \textbf{Empirical validation.} On Stable Diffusion (text-to-image), EDM~\citep{karras2022elucidating} (class-conditional), and flow-based generation, \textsc{GAINS} matches uniform-allocation quality with 20--50\% fewer NFE; the gains hold across local search operators, prompt sets, and model families (\cref{sec:experiments}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Related work.}
A growing body of work refines noise variables at generation time, with progressively richer local operators: $\epsilon$-greedy exploration~\citep{ramesh2025nts}, rollover budget forcing~\citep{kim2025rbf}, two-phase MCTS splits~\citep{zhang2025tscend}, adaptive cyclic diffusion~\citep{lee2025abcd}, candidate pruning~\citep{ttsnap2025}, Langevin MCMC with tree search~\citep{zhang2025classical}, and amortized noise prediction~\citep{eyring2025hypernetworks}. Particle-based methods~\citep{singhal2025fk,kim2025psi} allocate non-uniform particle budgets across denoising stages, and adaptive-compute methods~\citep{zhang2025adadiff,ye2025tpdm,yeh2025demons,sundaresha2025vt} modify per-step effort but primarily adapt the base sampler rather than scheduling additional search compute. These methods focus on \emph{local} search; \emph{how much} budget each step should receive has received less attention. Allocating a fixed budget across sequential stages is also a classical resource allocation problem~\citep{ibaraki1988resource}, with close connections to ranking and selection in simulation optimization~\citep{chen2000simulation} and to optimal importance sampling~\citep{lam2025importance}. We complement recent diffusion-theory results~\citep{gao2024reward,tang2024contractive,zhou2024jumpdiffusion} by deriving a per-step gain factorization that reduces budget allocation to a tractable water-filling form.
