\begin{abstract}
Diffusion models generate samples by traversing a denoising trajectory,
a sequence of noise-reduction steps that progressively transforms random noise
into data from the target distribution.
Allocating additional computation along this trajectory at inference time,
without retraining, can substantially improve the quality of the generated
samples.
However, existing methods distribute this computational budget uniformly or
with fixed heuristics across denoising steps, ignoring that different steps
differ markedly in their sensitivity to noise perturbations.
We formalize inference-time noise refinement as a two-level optimization
problem: a \emph{local} operator searches for better noise at a chosen
timestep, while a \emph{global} scheduler allocates per-timestep search
iterations under a strict budget of function evaluations.
Standard diffusion sampling, best-of-$N$
selection, and recent noise search methods emerge as special cases with fixed or
heuristic scheduling policies.
We propose \textbf{GAINS} (Global Adaptive Inference-time Noise Scheduling),
which combines offline sensitivity profiling with online
feedback control: offline analysis assigns a coarse per-step budget based on
estimated timestep importance, and an online controller applies windowed early
stopping driven by observed score gains and candidate variance, while enforcing
the exact compute budget.
Via a Taylor expansion of the verifier score along the diffusion SDE, we
derive a location-scale decomposition of per-step scores and obtain an explicit
formula for timestep sensitivity in terms of the diffusion coefficient and
the verifier gradient.
Under this structure, optimal allocation follows a water-filling rule
favoring high-sensitivity timesteps, and online adaptation can partially
recover a Jensen gap that any fixed allocation necessarily incurs.
On Stable Diffusion, EDM, and flow-based models, GAINS matches the uniform-allocation benchmark
with 20--50\% fewer function evaluations, and exceeds it at equal
budgets.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textit{Key words}: diffusion models; inference-time scaling; computational budget allocation; noise trajectory search; global scheduling; resource allocation; sequential decision making
